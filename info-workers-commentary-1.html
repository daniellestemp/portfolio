Information Workers: A Source of Accountability in an Information Civilization

Danielle Stemper | Oct 16, 2024

On the role of information workers in supporting transparency and accountability in a world deeply mediated by data. The summary below is a condensed overview — the full essay, with citations, lives on the original site.

Overview

Building on Shoshana Zuboff's idea of an "information civilization" — where existence itself is rendered and mediated by information — this essay explores what it means to work as an information professional today, using a 2012 Canadian internet-governance case as a lens.

It traces the concept of societal "Estates," from the clergy, nobility, and commons of feudal Europe to the press as a Fourth Estate, and then to a "Fifth Estate" of networked individuals empowered by the internet to hold institutions accountable. The case study centers on Canada's proposed Bill C-30: grassroots Twitter accounts surfaced information traditional media weren't covering, shaping public opinion and contributing to the bill being shelved within a year.

The essay argues that this kind of networked accountability is a defining feature of today's information civilization, one shaped by datafication and "surveillance capitalism." It closes by framing information work as inherently infrastructural and multidisciplinary — spanning research, design, data, and engineering — and therefore foundational to how that civilization holds itself accountable.
