Designing for Close-Knit Communities: Creating Informal Lending Functions for Venmo

The Challenge

Working within a given design brief — build an app, website, or extended feature for managing and tracking informal lending and borrowing among close-knit communities.

The team

Danielle Stemper (me), Sharron Lou, Tarun Venkatesan

We set out to understand, then design, digital features that would best accommodate informal lending — and concluded that close-knit communities need a design that takes both values and emotions into account.

The Solution: Venmo IOU

The team designed extended Venmo features for managing and tracking informal lending:

  • IOU Agreement System: a simple, shared record of the loan so neither side feels awkward or unsure about the terms.
  • Payment System: makes repayment straightforward, easing the stress of forgotten or late payments.
  • Reminder System: friendly, low-pressure nudges that lighten the emotional weight for both lender and borrower.

Videos & mock-ups by Sharron Lou.

How We Did It

Desk research

We explored the dynamics and emotional undercurrents of informal lending — trust, the incentives of informal vs. formal lending, and the role of FinTech and DeFi — and found that relationships, social capital, and technology all needed to be central to the design. Transparency and efficiency emerged as key values, alongside a clear-eyed look at what deters people from lending informally in the first place: borrowers open up to informal loans when they're comfortable with the process, and technology can help support that comfort.

Primary research

  • User interviews on lending/borrowing experiences and the role of digital technology in personal finance, surfacing the emotional dimensions of lending.
  • Observations of a recreational soccer league splitting costs for a night out, which clarified the logistics of sending and receiving money within a close-knit group.
  • Recurring themes: relationships, values, customs/habits, motivations, and de-motivations.

Conceptual development

Sketching and wireframe iterations shaped the final prototypes.

Engagement with designers

We gathered feedback throughout, culminating in a presentation to a class of design students. Key feedback: refine the reminder system with more customization for different relationship dynamics, explore mechanisms that encourage repayment accountability, and more clearly separate the borrow/lend flow from Venmo's existing pay/request flow.

Reflection & Key Learnings

The influence of emotions and social dynamics on the user experience turned out to be the common thread throughout informal lending.

  • Designing effective features means thinking about the user's experience beyond the application itself.
  • Social capital has a major influence on informal lending dynamics and often dictates the terms of an agreement.
  • A holistic approach helps you think outside the box, but knowing which tools fit your own process is just as essential to good design.
