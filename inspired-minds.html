DesignUsability Research

Creating an Engaging & Intuitive Experience for the Inspired Minds Art Center Website

The Inspired Minds Art Center

Located in Buda, Texas, Inspired Minds Art Center is a creative hub offering resources for artists, performers, and enthusiasts of all ages. Through Pratt Institute's Center for Digital Experiences, this project used moderated user testing to uncover usability issues on Inspiredminds.art ↗ and propose solutions.

The team

Danielle Stemper (me), Kshitija Tammireddi, Vasilios Nikolopoulos, Xin Yu Jiang

The Challenge

Our client had limited time and resources to dedicate to usability fixes, so we aimed to build a digestible, manageable guide — solutions that could be implemented short-term while setting up any longer-term efforts.

The goal

Create a client-friendly guide addressing the key usability issues on Inspiredminds.art.

The Process: A Moderated Remote User Test

We ran eight moderated remote user tests to surface usability issues on the site.

Kicking off the client relationship

We prioritized hearing directly from the client about feedback and issues they'd seen internally or from customers, and learned that our recommendations needed to be direct and manageable given their limited time and resources.

Building the test

Tasks focused on evaluating general navigation and more specific flows like classes and events, using a "think-aloud" method so participants could narrate their experience as they worked. Sessions were recorded via Zoom with notes taken live.

Recruiting target users

Our user profile — creative women, active parents ages 25–50 — guided recruitment through Private Panels and personal networks; all participants ultimately fit the profile and had experience with art classes or workshops.

Left: digital post-it synthesis of notes and insights from the user tests. Right: a site map built to clarify the existing structure and navigation.

The Results: Great Content, Room to Improve Navigation

Enhancing first impressions: users found the homepage header visually cluttered, so we proposed relocating certain items to the footer.

Cleaning up navigation: repetitive menu items confused users, so we proposed a consolidated sitemap.

Improving accessibility: we recommended color-palette adjustments to meet stronger contrast standards.

Recommendation 1: Move non-essential header elements to the footer

Before: overwhelming header information and an inconsistent image carousel. After: a balanced hero with a consistent baseline and uniform carousel images.

Recommendation 2: Remove duplicate menu items and reorganize navigation

Recommendation 3: Increase font weight and improve color contrast on event cards

"The team uncovered tangible recommendations in a useful guide for redesign."
— Client quote

We closed with a client presentation of our findings and mock-ups. The client was glad to see internal concerns validated and appreciated the actionable recommendations.

Reflections

Meet the client where they are: our client's less-technical background shaped how we structured the tests, thought about UX, and framed recommendations.

Manage expectations: the client had ambitious goals, and we had to balance that against project scope through clear, considerate communication.

What I'd change: I'd build in one more checkpoint with the client and team as a gut-check on the usability tasks before testing — an extra chance to align expectations given the client's experience level.
