Data, Information, Knowledge & the Three Paradigms of HCI

Danielle Stemper | Oct 19, 2024

Exploring foundational concepts of data, information, and knowledge alongside the three paradigms of Human-Computer Interaction. The summary below is a condensed overview — the full essay, with citations, lives on the original site.

Overview

The essay pairs Meadow and Yuan's definitions of data, information, and knowledge with Harrison, Tatar, and Sengers' three paradigms of HCI, using each framework to illuminate the other. Data aligns with the first paradigm's "objective," pragmatic fit between humans and machines; information aligns with the second paradigm's focus on generalizable models of how people and computers process meaning; and knowledge aligns with the third paradigm's emphasis on meaning-making across context, perspective, and experience.

Drawing on her own background in public relations, the author reflects on how notoriously hard it is to pin down a single definition for a field like PR — pointing to a 2012 industry-wide effort that combed through nearly a thousand submitted definitions before settling on one. She argues that, much like PR itself, Meadow and Yuan's data/information/knowledge framework and the three paradigms of HCI work best as flexible lenses rather than rigid categories — frameworks that can be adapted across a wide range of contexts rather than exhaustively defined once and for all.
